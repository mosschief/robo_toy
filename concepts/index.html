Tank, ready to build

Everything needed to build Tank v4: 15 printable parts, a wiring plan, firmware for the Nano and the camera board, and a phone control page. All of it is in the repo on the claude/robot-toy-concepts-twiow6 branch, with step-by-step instructions in docs/BUILD.md.

The robot

finalabout 216 × 222 × 300 mm15 printed parts

How it is built

Lower body
Tub on the chassis deck holding the battery, Nano, motor driver, 5 V converter and charger. A lid on top carries the torso and the mode button.
Torso
Shoulder and neck servos sit on internal bulkheads. The camera and distance sensor slide into rails behind the chest guard.
Head
Four 8×8 modules sit face-first in a carrier behind a 2 mm clear window. The back cap unscrews.
Arms
Each upper arm is two halves that clamp the elbow servo. The forearm has a fixed two-finger claw.
Back panel
Power switch, USB-C charging, PROG/RUN switch and Nano USB, all above the bumper.

Why it survives a kid

  • All five joints have a spring clutch: a hard yank makes the limb click round instead of stripping the servo gears.
  • Servos let go after 1.5 s of standing still, so pushing an arm by hand never fights the motor.
  • PETG body with 4 walls, TPU bumpers and antenna, and the screens sit behind clear windows.
  • Driving stops by itself 12 cm from a wall, and 0.4 s after the phone stops sending.
  • It charges through USB-C, so the batteries never come out.

Playing with it

Join the Wi-Fi network Tank-XXXX (password tankrobot) and open 192.168.4.1. The page shows the camera with a joystick and buttons for faces, sounds, arm poses and modes. It also shows the distance and battery readings.

Without a phone, the mode button on the lid switches modes, and the face shows the number:

Before you print

Measure the chassis

Chassis sizes vary between sellers. Set CH_* in hardware/config.scad and re-run export.sh. If the deck holes don't line up with the floor slots, drill new ones.

Check the LED modules

The face carrier fits the 32 × 32 mm modules (FC-16 style). If yours are the longer 50 × 32 mm boards, the carrier needs redrawing.

Print a clutch first

Print one clutch_hub and one forearm and test the click. If the click is too stiff or too loose, change the spring or CL_RH in config.scad.

Not tested on hardware yet

Both firmwares compile, and the parts were fit-checked in the 3D model, but nothing has been printed or powered on. Expect a little tuning: servo trims, the face module order and track direction are all in config.h.

What the camera adds

  • Live video to a phone or tablet over Wi-Fi, with on-screen driving buttons, so your kid sees what the robot sees
  • Simple vision tricks on the ESP32: follow a coloured ball, react when a face appears
  • The distance sensor brings back "stop before the wall" and lets it measure how far away the ball is

Trade-offs

  • The camera no longer turns with the head, so to look around the robot turns its whole body
  • The chest guard takes the hits the head used to avoid, so the lens sits behind a 2 mm clear cover recessed 3 mm

How the electronics split

ESP32-CAM: eyes and Wi-Fi

Runs the camera, the phone web page and any vision code. It sends short commands like "drive forward" or "happy face" to the Nano over a serial link (two wires, plus a two-resistor divider because the ESP32 is 3.3 V).

Otto Nano: body

Keeps doing what it does in Otto: servos, the 16×16 face, the buzzer, and now the motor driver. It also reads the VL53L0X over I2C (pins A4 and A5), so it can stop by itself even with Wi-Fi off. Programmed in the Arduino IDE exactly as before, so it still works on its own without the camera.

Bought chassis

The tracks, sprockets, road wheels and motors come as one tested unit. The printed body bolts to its aluminium deck with four M3 screws, and printed fenders cover the tracks so fingers stay out.

Power

2 × 18650 cells (7.4 V) in the chassis feed the motors through the driver, and a 5 V 3 A buck converter feeds the Nano, servos, matrices and camera.

Pin budget fits the Nano in every concept: all three matrices share one 3-wire chain, the motor driver takes 4 PWM pins (3, 5, 6, 11), the HC-SR04 2 pins, the buzzer 1, and servos go on the remaining pins. Otto's Bluetooth module would still fit for phone control.

How it stays in one piece

Arms that give way

Each arm attaches to its servo through a magnetic or spring-detent coupler, so a yank pops the arm off instead of stripping the servo gears. Arms get metal-gear MG90S servos, which are drop-in replacements for the SG90.

Soft outside, hard inside

Tyres, tracks, bumpers and the handle are TPU. The shell is PETG with 4 walls and 30% gyroid infill, not PLA, which goes brittle and cracks on drops.

Protected screens

The matrices sit recessed behind a 2 mm polycarbonate or PETG window, so little fingers and crashes never touch the LEDs.

Floating electronics

The Nano, driver and buck converter sit on one tray on foam pads, with heat-set brass inserts instead of screws threaded into plastic, so it can be opened and fixed many times.

Kid-safe power

Lithium cells live behind a screwed battery door with a protected charger. Any magnets are fully enclosed inside printed parts; for a child under about 6 I'd use the spring detent instead of magnets.

Programmable

Standard Arduino IDE (and Otto Blockly-style blocks later), with a small library of moves, faces and sounds so new tricks are a few lines of code.
